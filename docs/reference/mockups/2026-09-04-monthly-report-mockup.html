Poseidon · design mockup · September 2026

Monthly performance reports

This feature shows up in three separate places in the product, and each one is a section below. First the app itself, where a salesperson reads the report and asks questions about it. Then the email the team receives every month. Then the admin screens used to set a report up and send it. These are different parts of the product, not three designs of the same page.

Sample figures are synthetic and internally consistent. A design mockup, not live data.

Section 1 of 3 · in the app

User app

What a salesperson sees when they sign in to Poseidon. One screen, three columns, nothing to set up: their history on the left, the report in the middle, and a conversation on the right. The report opens by default, so the month’s numbers are the first thing on the screen.

Left — previous reports and conversations

Every report the user has access to, grouped by office, with each month that has been produced and whether it was sent, replaced or failed. Underneath, their previous conversations. One click reopens either.

Middle — the report view (opens here)

The month set against the month before: headline figures, ranked customers and ports, charts, the biggest movers with the context of what those accounts did elsewhere, and a written summary. Every number can be traced back to the query that produced it.

Right — chat about this report

A conversation that already knows which report is on screen, so follow-up questions can be asked in plain English without repeating the office or the dates. The tags above the message box show what an answer is filtered to; remove one to widen the question. Conversations are private to the person who asked.

Summary of operations

✓ Every number checked against the computed set

July 2026 → August 2026, Gibraltar Office. Overall volume increased by 5.6% (+2,350 tons), while gross profit decreased by 4.3% (−53,500). Margin moved from 29.54/ton to 26.76/ton (−2.78/ton). The office won 298 transactions in August vs 312 in July (14 fewer), with inquiries up 4.5% (531 vs 508). The win rate declined from 61.4% to 56.1%.

Key drivers

The GP decline is concentrated in one account. Meridian Bunkering's GP fell 56,500 (−26.3%) on volume, down 2,150 tons, while its margin actually improved to 30.92/ton. Northstar Lines offset a third of that with +29,300 (+20.5%), also volume-driven, on 1,150 more tons. Atlas Tankers added 6,300 purely on margin (27.58 → 30.03/ton). Corsair Maritime lost 3,300 on margin compression despite more volume.

Account context

Meridian Bunkering did not leave. Its whole-book GP is down only 12,400 (−3.1%): the volume that left Gibraltar reappeared under the Spain Office (+41,000), mostly at Algeciras (+31,200), supplied by the Med Supply Team (+28,400). Gibraltar's share of the account dropped from 53.4% to 40.6%. Northstar's gain is real growth for the account (+21,600 overall), with a small −7,700 offset at Rotterdam.

Insights

  • Margin compression is broad, not one deal: all three deal classes lost margin, CONTRACTED most sharply (31.29 → 25.43/ton).
  • TRADED business gained 800 tons but lost 53,700 of GP, so August's extra volume was priced thinner than July's.
  • Inquiries rose 4.5% while wins fell 4.5%; 37 more lost inquiries pushed the win rate down 5.3 points.
  • One account came in (Orion Offshore, 12,400 GP) and one went quiet (Kestrel Lines, 18,900 in July).

Section 2 of 3 · what lands in the inbox

Email

The monthly email the team receives, shown here the way it looks in Outlook. It goes to a named recipient list once a report admin has reviewed the report and pressed Send. The email carries the headline figures, the ranked tables and the written summary in plain tables that render correctly in any mail client, plus a PDF copy of the same thing. The heavier material — charts, account context, the full audit trail — stays in the app behind the “Open in Poseidon” button, which keeps the email short enough to read on a phone. Anyone who never opens the app still gets the month’s story.

Section 3 of 3 · report admins only

Setting up and sending a report

Three small forms, seen only by people with the report admin role. Between them they cover the whole life of a report: define it once, generate a month when you want it, and check who is on the list before anything leaves the building. Nothing is ever emailed without a person clicking Send.

Report definition

Set up once per office: which office, what it is compared against, and who receives it. Office names are picked from the values that actually exist in the data, so a report cannot be pointed at something that is not there.

Generate report

Choose a month and run it. It works in the background and lands as a draft in the sidebar in about a minute. Re-running a month replaces that draft and leaves anything already sent untouched.

Send report

The last check before it goes out: tick the recipients for this send, decide whether to attach the PDF, and confirm. Changes made here apply to this send only, not to the saved list.
